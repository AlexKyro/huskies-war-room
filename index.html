        What do these words and numbers mean?
        • Trouble Index — a score from 24 to 95 for how dangerous this opponent is to us specifically. It compares the situations where our offence struggles with where their defence is strongest, and adds how well their offence scores against the yards our defence gives up. It is not their record. Higher = harder game.
        • Our offence cards — the left and the top-right cards are about what our offence should do against their defence. The cards under "Their trigger men" are about their defence.
        • Success rate — the share of plays that gained enough to keep the drive alive: at least half the yards needed on 1st down, 60% on 2nd down, all of it on 3rd down. (The newer tabs — Who gets the ball, Cracks, Call sheet — use 60% on 2nd down, so numbers will differ slightly between tabs.)
        • Big plays — a run of 12+ yards or a pass of 20+ yards.
        • Yards per play — total yards divided by plays in that situation.
        • Plays / "% of our offence" — how many plays we ran in that situation, and what share of all our plays that is.
        • Predictability — how easy a situation is to guess, from 0 to 100. 0 means a coin flip (half runs, half passes); 100 means it is always the same call. Above about 60, the defence does not have to guess.
        • "1.40× normal" (load factor) — how many of their stops come from that situation compared with how often it comes up in football. 1.00 = normal. 1.40 = they make 40% more of their stops there than expected — they have decided to live there. Below 0.7 = they are thin there.
        • Stop and stop rate — a stop is a tackle on a play that failed (did not get the yards it needed). Stop rate is stops divided by all his tackles.
        • Disruption score — stop rate × the square root of his tackles × 10. It ranks defenders who both make many tackles and make them on failed plays, so a man cleaning up downfield does not rank high.
        • September and October — every play from both months counts the same. Games before September (the Aug 29–30 openers) are not included.

                What do these words and numbers mean?
                • Talent — built from three parts: frame (size against the position), production (his stats, adjusted for how much the team gave him) and impact (what the play-by-play shows). Scored 1 to 10. "Frame only" means he has no stats or charted plays yet, so his grade is pulled halfway back toward 5.
                • Intelligence and Work — you grade these 1 to 10 from film: Intelligence is how often he busts assignments; Work is whether he loafs in any of the four quarters.
                • Total (/30) — Talent + Intelligence + Work. The gap between the two totals decides the call at the bottom, from "Mismatch — attack it" to "Losing matchup — scheme around it".
                • The Fulcrum — team-level scores (health, bench depth, coaching, starters) that limit how far the board can tip and whether an edge still holds in the fourth quarter. You enter them once a week.

                What do these words and numbers mean?
                • Big number (% runs) — the share of plays that were runs in that down and distance. 50 is a coin flip. A high number means they nearly always run; a low number means they nearly always throw.
                • Colour — red = runs 72% or more (easy to read for the defence), gold = runs 28% or less (almost always throws), white = mixed.
                • Short, Medium, Long — 1–3 yards to go, 4–7 yards to go, 8 or more yards to go.
                • Predictability — how easy a situation is to guess, from 0 to 100. 0 means a coin flip (half runs, half passes); 100 means it is always the same call. Above about 60, the defence does not have to guess.
                • Success, big plays, yards per play — success rate: the share of plays that gained enough to keep the drive alive: at least half the yards needed on 1st down, 60% on 2nd down, all of it on 3rd down. (The newer tabs — Who gets the ball, Cracks, Call sheet — use 60% on 2nd down, so numbers will differ slightly between tabs.) Big plays: a run of 12+ yards or a pass of 20+ yards.
                • Tells — the situations where the offence is easiest to guess. A defence can lean on these.

                What do these words and numbers mean?
                • Tkl — tackles he made.
                • Stops — tackles he made on plays that failed to get the yards needed.
                • Stop rate — stops divided by tackles. Green = 55% or more, red = under 40%.
                • Disruption — stop rate × the square root of his tackles × 10. Rewards making tackles that kill drives, not just tackles.
                • Run% — the share of his tackles that came on runs.
                • Lives — the part of the field where most of his tackles happen.
                • By zone / down / quarter — small bars. Taller bar = more of his tackles there. By zone runs from our goal line (left) to the opponent’s goal line (right); by down is nine down-and-distance groups from 1st-and-short to 3rd-and-long; by quarter is Q1 to Q4.
                • Carrier table — touches = carries plus catches; per touch = yards per touch (green 8+, red under 5); big plays = a run of 12+ yards or a pass of 20+ yards.

                Who actually makes the play

                Tackle totals flatter the man cleaning up five yards downfield. Stop rate is the share of a defender's tackles that landed on a play which failed — the ones that killed the down. Disruption multiplies that by volume, so it rewards the man who makes a lot of tackles that actually hurt. The bars are where his work happens: by field zone, then by down and distance.

                What do these words and numbers mean?
                • Chance a play works — the big number in each box. A play "works" if it gains about half the yards needed on 1st down, 60% on 2nd down, or all of it on 3rd down.
                • + / − small number — points above or below what an average matchup would give. Green = good for the Huskies offence.
                • n 32/20 — 32 plays of ours and 20 plays of theirs in that situation. Fewer than 5 of ours shows grey: no read.
                • Edge — how much better or worse our offence should do against this defence than against an average one. Positive is good.
                • Strength — average points scored minus points allowed per game. Bigger = better team.
